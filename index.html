About

Fading Athens is a photo project by Steven A.J. Beijer, a Dutch-American photographer and urban archaeologist.

It looks at the parts of the city that rarely make it onto a postcard: the neighbourhood barber, the kiosk selling batteries and watches, the shop whose name is painted by hand above a shutter that may not open again. The project records them as they are, while they are still there.

Steven was born in the Netherlands, grew up partly in California and has lived in six countries. He got his first camera at around ten. In 1996, after finishing school in a small Dutch town, he came to Athens to study at Deree. He planned to stay a year and stayed five, spending his first year as a DJ at Club Decadence and falling for the city straight away. He came back in 2005 and lived in Athens for another five years before moving to Barcelona in 2010. Athens remains his happy place and his muse: these days he is back almost every other month, staying in Kypseli, his favourite neighbourhood.

Since 1996 he has watched Athens lose its signs, facades, old shops, arcades, buses and trolleys. He calls Fading Athens a love letter to the city and an archive at the same time.

«Ίσως δεν μπορούμε να τα σώσουμε αλλά τουλάχιστον έχουμε μία οπτική ανάμνηση από αυτά.»

Maybe we can’t save them, but at least we have a visual memory of them.

To him the city is “a large, messy, living creature” that never stops changing, and Fokionos Negri is “a giant multicultural living room”. His walks run through Kypseli, Pedion tou Areos and Exarcheia, out past the Peace and Friendship Stadium to the coast and central Piraeus, and up onto the Pnyx.

Quotes from Steven’s interview with Athens Voice, 2020.
