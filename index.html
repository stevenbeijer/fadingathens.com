Η Αθήνα που σβήνει

Hand-painted letters, rolled-down shutters, the last of the old Athens, kept before they go dark.

The small streets, corners and shops of Athens: barbershops, kiosks and cafés that kept the same lettering for decades. A photo project by Steven A.J. Beijer.

Γράμματα ζωγραφισμένα στο χέρι, κατεβασμένα ρολά, ό,τι απέμεινε από την παλιά Αθήνα, πριν σβήσει.

Τα μικρά δρομάκια, οι γωνιές και τα μαγαζιά της Αθήνας: κουρεία, περίπτερα και καφενεία που κρατούν τα ίδια γράμματα εδώ και δεκαετίες.

About

Fading Athens is a photo project founded in 2019 by Steven A.J. Beijer, a Dutch photographer and urban archaeologist.

It looks at the parts of the city that rarely make it onto a postcard: the neighbourhood barber, the kiosk selling batteries and watches, the shop whose name is painted by hand above a shutter that may not open again. The project records them as they are, while they are still there.

Το Fading Athens είναι ένα φωτογραφικό πρότζεκτ που ξεκίνησε το 2019 ο Steven A.J. Beijer, Ολλανδοαμερικανός φωτογράφος και αστικός αρχαιολόγος. Καταγράφει ό,τι σπάνια βλέπει κανείς σε καρτ ποστάλ: το κουρείο της γειτονιάς, το περίπτερο, το μαγαζί με τη ζωγραφισμένη στο χέρι ταμπέλα πάνω από ένα ρολό που ίσως δεν ξανανοίξει. Ένα ερωτικό γράμμα στην πόλη και, ταυτόχρονα, ένα αρχείο της.

Steven was born in the Netherlands, grew up partly in California and has lived in six countries. He got his first camera at around ten. In 1996, after finishing school in a small Dutch town, he came to Athens to study at Deree. He planned to stay a year and stayed five. He fell for the city straight away and started DJing at Club Decadence, where he became a resident DJ and played for the rest of his time there. He came back in 2005 and lived in Athens for another five years before moving to Barcelona in 2010. Athens remains his happy place and his muse: these days he is back almost every other month, staying in Kypseli, his favourite neighbourhood.

Since 1996 he has watched Athens lose its signs, facades, old shops, arcades, buses and trolleys. He calls Fading Athens a love letter to the city and an archive at the same time.

«Ίσως δεν μπορούμε να τα σώσουμε αλλά τουλάχιστον έχουμε μία οπτική ανάμνηση από αυτά.»

Maybe we can’t save them, but at least we have a visual memory of them.

To him the city is “a large, messy, living creature” that never stops changing, and Fokionos Negri is “a giant multicultural living room”. His walks run through Kypseli, Pedion tou Areos and Exarcheia, out past the Peace and Friendship Stadium to the coast and central Piraeus, and up onto the Pnyx.

Quotes from Steven’s interview with Athens Voice, 2020.

A Journey Through Athens’ Fading Beauty

Forgotten alleys, old bars, faded signs, human stories and overlooked architecture.

Walk the streets behind the photographs with Steven, from the National Archaeological Museum through Omonia’s arcades to Monastiraki and the Central Market: the old shopfronts, hand-painted signs and abandoned hotels, seen on foot before they disappear.
